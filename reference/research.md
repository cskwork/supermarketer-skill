# Research playbook

Research exists to support a decision, not to create a large generic report.

## Frame the research question

Record:

- decision to be made,
- target audience/market,
- geography/language,
- freshness requirement,
- acceptable evidence,
- time/scope cap.

## Evidence priority

1. approved first-party product/customer data,
2. official platform/policy/company sources,
3. primary studies and datasets,
4. reputable secondary analysis,
5. user-provided evidence,
6. low-confidence directional sources only when clearly labeled.

## Required fields per evidence item

- evidence ID,
- fact or observation,
- source,
- publication/checked date,
- relevant scope,
- confidence,
- limitations,
- decision relevance.

## Research streams

Use only what the objective needs:

- product truth,
- customer/problem language,
- buying process,
- category/competitor/substitute,
- channel behavior/specification,
- trend pulse,
- policy/rights/regulation,
- post-launch analytics.

## Rules

- Separate fact, inference, recommendation, and assumption.
- Do not infer demand from search volume alone.
- Do not infer product fit from competitor existence alone.
- Do not use current competitor price/spec from memory.
- Record conflicts and unknowns.
